R1 — apply a match decision to every recipe with that ingredient

The server now reuses a person's match decision across recipes (the same ingredient item appears in two or more recipes for 88% of all lines in the library). At compute time this is automatic. This page is the one place a person chooses to push a decision out right away: an offer that appears under a line the moment it has been decided, sized by the server's count of what would change. Nothing here is a checkbox to remember to tick before saving — the count for the new food only exists after the pick has landed.

2 · While it runs, and what it says when it lands

The apply is one request; on the real library it finishes in well under a second (measured: 456 recipes, 522 lines, 113 ms). The strip is the progress and the receipt — no modal, no page change. The sheet stays open so the admin keeps reviewing. A toast is not added on top: the strip already says it.

Nothing to offer — the common case, and the only state most rows will ever be in

A pick or a confirm whose food every other recipe already carries (or an item used nowhere else) shows no strip at all: others: 0. The offer never appears for a Skip, and never for a grams-only change — the server refuses to broadcast those, and the UI does not ask.

3 · “Confirm as-is” gets the same offer

5 · What the app needs from the server (all shipped)
Countothers on every line of GET …/nutrition/matches — recipes with an unreviewed line of the item not already on this line's food. The PUT returns the same list, so the number is fresh right after a pick or a confirm.
ApplyPUT …/matches/{pos} with {fdc_id, apply_to_all: true} (or {confirmed: true, apply_to_all: true}) — the same decision, sent again with the flag. A grams-only or skip request with the flag is refused with a 422 and nothing changes.
Receiptapplied: {recipes, lines, failed} in that PUT's response — exact counts of what was written; failed names how many recipes could not be recomputed.
ReversalNone needed: the rows land as unreviewed (auto) at full confidence, so a second apply with a different food reaches every one of them.
Ingredient nameThe strip names the item (“unsalted butter”), not the whole line — the app has the parsed line from the recipe it is showing.
Not in this pass

A library-wide “decisions” page listing every item with a human decision and its reach; a per-item undo history; the review queue surfacing items whose decisions disagree between recipes (with propagation now auto everywhere, the newest decision simply wins). Each is a separate item if wanted.
